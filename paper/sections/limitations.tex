\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{One deep case.} The equal-evidence comparisons rest mainly on one dataset (Banking77) at one shot count.
The retrieval findings are measured on it, and the factorial analysis of what in the retrieval rule matters is post
hoc. \pending{Bitext and the 13-task block reduce, but do not remove, this limitation.}

\paragraph{Fast-moving systems.} The decision models evaluated here are versioned products; later versions may
behave differently. We pin every version, record it per call or per run, and release the request bodies so that new
versions can be scored on the same evidence.

\paragraph{Training data cannot be verified.} The protocol equalises inputs, not training data. Exposure is recorded
from documentation; where documentation is missing, results are marked undisclosed, and absence of a dataset from a
manifest does not prove absence from training.

\paragraph{Calibration metrics.} ECE with equal-width bins depends on the binning; we use one scheme throughout and
also report the Brier score. Log loss is not usable for systems that round their probabilities.

\paragraph{Baselines are not tuned for leaderboards.} \orb{} and \gj{} were built as low-cost, documented testbeds.
They are not the strongest open systems we evaluate, and the fine-tuning reference is not converged.

\section{Broader impact}
Better comparisons of decision models help practitioners choose systems for routing and triage, where errors and
over-confident probabilities affect people. The same evidence could be used to market one system over another; we
report every result we pre-registered, including those unfavourable to our own baselines.

\section{Release}
\label{sec:release}
We release, under the licences of their sources, every request body with its hash manifest, every per-item
prediction and probability of every system, the pre-registration documents with their commit history, and the
scripts that generate every number in this paper from those files. Per-item files containing test utterances keep
the licences of their datasets (share-alike for MTOP and Bitext). Closed-API outputs are released as measurements;
