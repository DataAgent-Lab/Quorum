\section{The zero-shot jury in full}
\label{app:jury}

\paragraph{Design.} \gj{} scores each utterance against every label text (name or one-line description) with three
encoders: an NLI cross-encoder, which scores the entailment of the hypothesis ``This message is about
\emph{label}.'' given the utterance, and two bi-encoders, which score the cosine similarity between the utterance and
the same templated hypothesis. Each member's scores pass through a softmax with temperature one, and the members are pooled by an
equal-weight geometric mean. No weight or temperature is fitted per task.

\paragraph{The jury against its members.} With the original NLI member, the jury beats its best single member on
\zsWinsNames{} of 7 datasets with label names and \zsWinsDesc{} of 7 with descriptions (exact McNemar, Holm over
14). With the documented-clean NLI member it wins on \zxCleanNliWins{} of 14 and on all
\zxCleanNliUnexposedOf{} conditions that are unexposed for every member. With a weaker NLI member trained only on
SNLI and MNLI the picture is mixed, so the benefit of pooling depends on member quality.

\paragraph{Descriptions vs.\ names.} One-line descriptions written by an LLM from the label names alone improve the
jury on all seven datasets, significantly on six (all but HWU64, $p_\text{Holm}=\zsHwuDescVsNamePHolm$; exact
McNemar, Holm over the seven), most on MTOP (\zsMtopDescVsNameGain{} points) and SNIPS
(\zsSnipsDescVsNameGain), whose label names are terse identifiers.

\paragraph{Combination rule.} The geometric mean is more accurate than the arithmetic mean on \zxGeoGtArith{} of 14
conditions and than majority vote on \zxGeoGtVote{} of 14. Of these differences, \zxGeoSigArith{} against the
arithmetic mean and \zxGeoSigVote{} against the vote are significant (exact McNemar, Holm over the 28 tests); no
condition favours either alternative significantly (\zxArithSigGeo{} and \zxVoteSigGeo).

\paragraph{Calibration with one temperature.} The raw jury is under-confident on all \zxRawUnderconfident{}
conditions (ECE \zxRawEceMin--\zxRawEceMax; on Banking77 with names, mean confidence \zxBankingNameRawConf{} at
accuracy \zsBankingNameAcc\%), because the two bi-encoders produce nearly flat distributions and the geometric mean
inherits their flatness. A single global temperature, fitted once on a pre-registered sample of training-split
utterances across tasks ($T^*=\zxTstarOrig$), lowers test ECE on \zxTstarOrigBetter{} of \zxTstarOrigOf{} conditions
(e.g.\ Banking77 names \zxBankingNameRawEce{} to \zxBankingNameTstarEce; MTOP names \zxMtopNameRawEce{} to
\zxMtopNameTstarEce), with non-overlapping bootstrap intervals on \zxTstarCiDisjoint{} and accuracy unchanged by
construction. The same procedure, run once per jury, gives the same result for the clean-member jury
($T^*=\zxTstarCleanNli$, \zxTstarCleanNliBetter/\zxTstarCleanNliOf) and the SNLI/MNLI jury ($T^*=\zxTstarSnliNli$,
\zxTstarSnliNliBetter/\zxTstarSnliNliOf).
