Zero-shot, the decision models lead by wide margins (Table~\ref{tab:zeroshot}). In the pre-registered comparison
against \gj{} with the clean NLI member, \jev{} is ahead on every condition (Holm over four primary conditions:
Banking77 and MTOP, $p_\text{Holm}\le\fsZsBankingDescJevPHolm$; Holm over six further conditions on CLINC150, SNIPS
and Bitext, $p_\text{Holm}\le\fsZsSnipsDescJevPHolm$). \pplx{} is higher than \jev{} on every condition where both were run (with different request wording), and \clef{} reaches
\zxClincNameClefBigAcc\% on CLINC150 with label names (the only completed \clef{} zero-shot cell).

How to read these numbers is the point of this subsection. Only one column is interpretable as zero-shot for every
dataset: \gj{} with the clean NLI member, whose three members document their training data and exclude all seven
benchmarks. The other columns mix three cases. Some cells are \emph{exposed}: \pplx's training manifest lists
MASSIVE, MTOP and SNIPS, and our own original NLI member inherits the Banking77 and MASSIVE training splits through
its initialisation (\S\ref{sec:find-exposure}). Some are \emph{undisclosed}: \jev{} and \clef{} do not document their
training data, and the remaining \pplx{} cells are not covered by its manifest. Some are \emph{indirect}: HWU64 and
MASSIVE share a large fraction of their utterances. We therefore report decision-model zero-shot numbers as
measurements of these systems on these test sets, not as evidence of zero-shot generalisation.
\authorcheck{The public leaderboard gap for \clef{} on CLINC150 (public vs.\ private set) is reported by a third
party and not verified here; decide whether to mention it.}

Within \gj, the internal findings that motivated it hold with the clean member: the jury beats its best single
member on \zxCleanNliWins{} of 14 conditions (Holm), including all \zxCleanNliUnexposedOf{} conditions on datasets that
are unexposed for every member, and loses on \zxCleanNliLosses. Appendix~\ref{app:jury} reports the jury in full.
